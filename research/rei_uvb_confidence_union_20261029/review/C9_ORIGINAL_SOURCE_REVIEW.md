## Independent code-to-model derivation

Let `r=d-m`, `x=z-3.6`, `X=[1,x]`, `M=X^T C^-1 X`. Dimensions are `C:7x7`, `X:7x2`, `M:2x2`, `A=M^-1 X^T C^-1:2x7`. The minimized residual is `r+theta*v+j-X*(beta,kappa)`, hence the fitted parameter vector is

`a=A*r+(A*v)*theta+A*j`.

The sign of each correction is positive on the observation side; `theta=(gamma_TDR-1.4)/0.2`. A positive source correction does not imply positive slope correction: the slope row has positive and negative weights. The source's gamma=1.6 therefore corresponds to theta=+1, which the saved signed response maps to decreasing kappa. Symmetric theta support alone cannot detect a reversed physical label.

For `jc=(lo+hi)/2`, `jh=(hi-lo)/2`, the image is `center + sum(g_i*q_i)`, where `center=A*(r+jc)`, `g_0=A*v`, `g_(i+1)=A[:,i]*jh_i`, and each `q_i` lies in [-1,1]. Thus direction `s` has support `s*center+sum(abs(s*g_i))`. Each coordinate minimum/maximum is its center minus/plus the corresponding absolute sum. Endpoint selection by coefficient sign attains the bounds; a zero coefficient can choose either endpoint without affecting the value. The code's zero-coefficient tie convention is valid.

Inputs `r,v,j,beta` have dex units; `x` is redshift, kappa is dex per redshift, and C is dex squared. The full correlated C is used throughout. Its inverse defines dimensionless chi-square. Conditional parameter covariance is `M^-1`; the reported `var_kappa=M[0,0]/det(M)` has the corresponding squared slope units. Beta sigma is explicitly null, not a computed uncertainty.
